Triangulation Coloring Optimization

Fixed triangulation_coloring_optimization

The grader allows mesh moves the instructions forbid, and its test inputs overlap.

SummaryThe instructionsHow it is gradedWhat we foundFiles

Summary

The task
Starting from a triangle mesh (a network of triangles) over 512 points, recolour points and swap shared triangle edges to get the lowest possible score.
What goes wrong
The instructions allow swapping an edge only when its two triangles form a convex four-sided shape. The grader skips that condition, so it accepts swaps that fold the mesh over itself. The same rule built the hidden inputs: all ten contain overlapping triangles. A forbidden swap can improve the score, as one did on a hidden case. Separately, the grader runs 8 cases at once under a 30-second wall-clock limit; on the publisher's 4-CPU setting a valid solution using 20 seconds of CPU per case timed out on 8 of 10 cases, which leaves the submission without a score.
How much it matters
Changes which moves are allowed for every solver; how much a strong solver gains from forbidden moves was not measured. On a 4-CPU grader, solutions using about 15 to 30 seconds of CPU per case lost their whole score.
What we did
Fixed in a new version (v2.2): forbidden flips are rejected, the test inputs are regenerated as complete triangulations (no overlaps, no gaps at the edge of the point set; the previous fixed version left gaps in 12 of 13 files), and the grader runs no more cases at once than it has CPUs. Scores are not comparable with the original.
Validation status
Convexity, CPU and convex-hull coverage validated: all 13 v2.2 inputs cover their hull exactly (exact census); three identical paired runs of the original, v2.1 and v2.2 graders.
Formal check
Proved in Lean 4. The repaired flip rule matches strict convexity for one modeled flip and rejects the demonstrated folding flip. The proof does not cover the meshes; full convex-hull coverage of the 13 regenerated v2.2 inputs is checked by an exact-arithmetic census, not in Lean. Limit: One flip, not the whole mesh. Hull coverage (13 of 13 v2.2 files; v2.1 left gaps in 12) and CPU behavior checked by exact scripts and runs..

Triangulation Coloring - Minimize Ugliness (CodeChef TRICOL)

Write solution.py in the project root (stdin → stdout). All modifications must be made only in solution.py.

Read README.md, tools/README.md, and sample_data/README.md. Official scoring: flips change the triangulation; U (ugly triangles) is counted on the final triangulation after all flips.

Evaluation

  • 10 hidden judge cases, each N=512
  • G = X·C + Y·F + U² per case; total score = sum (lower is better)

Local Testing

Use the 3 pre-built N=512 files in sample_data/. Use only these pre-built cases; do not call tools/src/gen.py or generate random local cases.

./eval_sample_data.sh
# or per file:
python3 solution.py < sample_data/0000.txt > output.txt
./tools/bin/tester sample_data/0000.txt output.txt

Also try sample_data/0001.txt and sample_data/0002.txt.

When ./eval_sample_data.sh improves, submit for all 10 hidden judge cases.

Strategy (algorithm exploration)

Focus on improving solution.py using sample_data/ for feedback:

  1. Joint optimization — recolor and flip interact; optimize them together, not in tiny fixed phases.
  2. U² dominates on hard cases — reducing ugly triangles by even a few often beats tweaking C/F.
  3. Iterate: change algorithm → ./eval_sample_data.sh → submit when promising.
  4. Do not loop local tests endlessly; move to submit after a few sample checks.

Rules

  • Only modify solution.py; do not change tools/ or sample_data/
  • Output: line 1 C F, line 2 color string (length N), then F flip lines (1-indexed vertex pairs)
  • The local tester applies flips exactly like the judge

What we found

The full case: the test submissions, the independent checks and the fix. File paths refer to the repository; links open on GitHub.

Outcome: Fixed. The grader's rule for legal edge flips leaves out the README's convexity condition; the same rule generated all ten hidden inputs, which contain overlapping or folded triangles, and it also decides which submitted moves are accepted. Separately, on the publisher's 4-CPU grader a valid solution using more than about 15 s of CPU per case times out, although the README promises 30 s per case, and one timeout leaves the submission without a score; the fixed version also runs at most as many cases at once as the grader has CPUs.

The fixed version is at package v2.2. Its regenerated inputs cover their convex hull exactly, as a triangulation of the points must: an exact census finds 0 gaps and 0 overlaps in all 13 files. The previous fixed version (v2.1) left 1–3 hull triangles missing in 12 of 13 regenerated files; the paired runs found no wrong or unrepeatable score from that gap, and v2.1 and v2.2 grade the same inputs identically.

What the task asks

The input is "a triangulation" of N = 512 points: a mesh of triangles with corners at the points that covers their convex hull (the smallest convex region containing them) without gaps or overlaps. The agent recolours points and flips edges to lower a score. The README defines the move: "if an edge is a diagonal of a convex quadrilateral formed by two adjacent triangles, replace it with the other diagonal. The result remains a valid triangulation."

The prompt says "The local tester applies flips exactly like the judge". The score is counted "on the final triangulation after all flips". Category: Combinatorial Optimization.

What goes wrong

Rule. A flip is legal only for a diagonal of a convex quadrilateral. The shared library's Triangulation.is_flippable checks only that the two apexes (the far corners of the two triangles) lie on opposite sides of the shared edge. It never checks that the shared edge's endpoints lie on opposite sides of the new diagonal. It therefore accepts flips of non-convex quadrilaterals, and those flips fold the mesh.

Effect on the inputs. gen.py applies K random flips with this rule to build every case. All ten hidden inputs contain overlapping triangles, with 17 to 583 overlapping triangle pairs per case. Six of them also contain duplicate triangles and non-manifold edges (edges shared by more than two triangles). Plain generator output with no flips (K=0) has no overlaps.

Effect on scoring. The same rule is the official grader's move rule:

  • On every hidden case, 317–379 interior edges pass the official rule but are not diagonals of a convex quadrilateral. The README forbids flipping these edges.
  • No convex-quadrilateral flip is rejected. The official rule allows everything the documented rule allows, and more.
  • Example under the original grader (case 0009): the shipped do-nothing output scores Score = 644809 (U=803). Adding one README-forbidden non-convex flip (edge 39–52) is accepted and scores Score = 641611 (U=801). A forbidden move improves the official score (lower is better).
  • Minimal example (N=4): the input is a valid two-triangle mesh whose quadrilateral has a reflex (inward-pointing) corner. The original tester accepts the forbidden flip (Score = 41), and the resulting two triangles overlap. The problem does not depend on a bad input.

The 30 s per case shrinks to about 15 s of CPU on a 4-CPU grader. The README says "Time limit: 30 seconds per case" (line 50). The grader /tmp/eval_triangulation_coloring.py runs 8 cases at once (max_workers=min(8, len(case_files)), line 44) under a 30 s wall-clock limit (TIME_LIMIT = 30, line 9); a timed-out case scores float('inf') (line 36), which leaves the submission without a score out of 100 (sforge/harness/score_rescale.py:75-76). The task sets no CPU count. In EdgeBench's leaderboard configuration examples/all-tasks-k8s/experiment.yaml (quoted in evidence/impact/edgebench-leaderboard-config.md) (sha256 d9d299794ba946cae8d2b7f68a7879a35bb8565d95e78107edf79802993643be), the grader inherits judge_cpu_limit: 4, so each of the first 8 cases gets about half a CPU. Measured on the original grader at --cpus 4 (evidence/runs/triangulation_coloring_optimization-cpu4v8-a/result/out/summary.txt, -b) with the honest greedy.py control followed by fixed CPU work: 12 or 14 s per case passes 10/10; 20 s per case gives 2 OK, 8 TLE and TOTAL_SCORE inf on both workers. At --cpus 8 the 20 s solution passes 10/10 with 866,166 (48.87 out of 100). A solution that stops at a 25 s wall-clock deadline gets a mean of 15.00 s of CPU per case at 4 CPUs (median 12.62 s; 2 of 10 cases run alone at the end) and 24.97 s at 8 CPUs.

What limits the harm. The agent's working image ships the same verifier.py, tester and samples. The agent's local testing therefore sees exactly the grader's rule, so scores are consistent and repeatable. The harm is that the scored problem differs from the documented one.

How much it matters

CPU count. At the publisher's 4 CPUs a valid solution using about 15–30 s of CPU per case loses its whole score under the original grader (measured with 20 and 28 s per case). Under the fixed version it is scored. A solution that stops at a wall-clock deadline gets about twice the CPU per case under the fixed version (mean 15.00 s → 24.91 s for a 25 s deadline); how much that changes its score is not measured.

Flip rule. The rule changes which flips are legal for every solver, and the hidden inputs are folded for every solver. The only score effect measured is one forbidden flip on case 0009, which lowers that case from 644,809 to 641,611 (the 10-case total is about 6.09 million for a do-nothing output). The honest greedy test submission moves from 48.87 to 48.66 out of 100 under the fixed version v2.2 (48.95 under v2.1), on different inputs. The size of the effect for a strong solver over a full optimization was not measured.

The fix

The fixed version changes is_flippable in tools/src/triangulation.py, in both the grader image and the agent's working image. It now also requires a != b, that edge ab does not already exist, and that the two shared-edge endpoints lie strictly on opposite sides of the new diagonal. Nothing else in the file changes.

The fixed version also changes tools/src/delaunay.py (package v2.2). The shipped generator builds its Delaunay mesh inside a finite "super-triangle" and so drops 1–3 triangles next to the convex hull in most inputs; the result is a mesh of a polygon through the points, not a triangulation of the points. delaunay_triangles() now ends with complete_hull(), which fills each such pocket by ear clipping and then restores the Delaunay property with Lawson flips, using exact integer orientation and in-circle tests. On the 13 seeds this adds 0–3 triangles before any pre-flip and changes no existing triangle (v2/complete_hull_check.jsonl).

The 10 hidden cases and 3 samples were then regenerated with the shipped, unmodified gen.py, the v2.2 delaunay.py and the fixed rule:

  • Case 0000 and the samples keep their original seeds (9000000 and 1000001–1000003).
  • Cases 0001–0009 use new seeds 9000001–9000009, because the original seeds are unknown. K is inferred from each case's pattern of defects.
  • Case 0000 had no hull gap and is byte-identical to the previous fixed version (v2.1). The other 12 files are new instances compared with v2.1.

The fixed version also changes /tmp/eval_triangulation_coloring.py so that it runs at most as many cases at once as the grader's CPU quota allows: a new function cpu_quota_workers(), the same as in repairs/grid_turing_robot-v2/, reads the cgroup quota (cpu.max, or the cgroup v1 quota and period), rounds it down to whole CPUs, falls back to os.cpu_count() without a quota, and keeps the result between 1 and 8; the pool becomes ProcessPoolExecutor(max_workers=min(cpu_quota_workers(), len(case_files))). With 8 or more CPUs it runs 8 cases at once, exactly as the original.

These are unchanged: verifier.py, tester, gen.py, the READMEs, the prompt, the parser, the 30 s limit per case, the 1200 s eval_timeout and the reference points of the score scale.

  • On the 13 fixed inputs (v2.1 and v2.2), the fixed rule equals a strictly-convex-quadrilateral test on every edge. It allows only flips the old rule allowed, and it forbids no flip the README allows. The Lean proof covers every valid triangulation.
  • The comparison test in v2/rule_check.py also contains the fixed rule's two new guards (a != b, and edge ab does not already exist), so on its own it cannot show that those guards reject nothing. Without them, the convexity counts in flip_rules.jsonl (convex_flippable) exceed the rule_check.jsonl counts on every original hidden case. The fixed rule would reject 2, 1, 2, 2, 2, 19, 17, 24, 20 and 23 on hidden cases 0000–0009 strictly convex flips whose other diagonal already exists in the folded mesh (for example case 0009, edge 79–156 with apexes 15 and 497). Such edges cannot occur in a valid triangulation, and the fixed version ships regenerated, valid inputs, so this does not affect the fixed version. It means the rule fix alone, applied to the original inputs, would forbid a few flips of strictly convex quadrilaterals, whose result would duplicate an existing edge.
  • On the original inputs the rule fix removes 319–398 flippable edges per hidden case (original minus fixed counts in rule_check.jsonl). This is slightly more than the 317–379 non-convex edges above, by the guarded convex edges just described.

Score compatibility: fixed-version scores are not comparable with original scores, for four reasons:

  • 9 of 10 hidden inputs are new instances (and v2.2 instances differ from v2.1 ones, so v2.2 scores are not comparable with v2.1 scores either).
  • All inputs are now valid triangulations of their convex hull.
  • The set of legal moves is smaller.
  • At fewer than 8 grader CPUs a solution that searches until a wall-clock deadline gets more CPU per case (about twice at 4 CPUs).

All four original reference points of the score scale are kept unchanged, as a fixed reference scale only. They no longer correspond to leaderboard ranks on the fixed version. The v2.2 do-nothing total (6,108,380) lies 0.1% above the original baseline (6,102,378), so it scores 0.000 instead of 0.055.

How we checked it

  • Original grader. The original tools/bin/tester and the full original eval_cmd ran in the original public image, pinned by digest, with --network none, for the original and fixed versions.
  • Independent check. Exact integer geometry, with no floating point, ran on all 10 hidden cases and all 3 samples. It covers: an exact separating-axis overlap test for each triangle pair; proper edge crossings; vertices inside triangles; duplicate, degenerate and non-manifold triangles and edges; Euler characteristic and vertex links (two standard checks that a mesh is a proper surface); area excess against the convex hull; and collinear triples. On every interior edge, the official rule was compared with an independent exact convex-quadrilateral test.
  • Regeneration. The shipped gen.py with seed 9000000 reproduces hidden case 0000 byte for byte. Seeds 1000001–1000003 reproduce all three samples byte for byte. (The O(N³) collinearity check was swapped for an exactly equivalent faster test.)
  • Convex-hull coverage. v2/hull_check.py checks each input with integer orientation and in-circle tests and exact rational (fractions.Fraction) areas: no overlapping interiors, total triangle area equal to the hull area, boundary edges equal to hull edges, and 2N−2−h triangles. It ran locally and, with each image's own Python, on the files inside the original, v2.1 and v2.2 images (evidence/runs/triangulation-v22-geom, status: exit=0); the records are identical. v2/complete_hull_check.jsonl checks the generator change before any pre-flip.
  • Repeatability. The side-by-side run of the original and fixed graders was repeated three times; no verdict changed (data/paired-repeatability.md). The v2.2 paired run of the original grader, v2.1 and v2.2 (evidence/runs/triangulation-v22-paired, -rep2, -rep3) ran three times on fresh E2B workers, each status: exit=0, with identical counts and totals.
  • CPU runs and paired run on E2B. Fixed-work solutions (12 to 28 s per case), a 25 s wall-clock-deadline solution and a 40 s bad control ran on the original grader at --cpus 4 and --cpus 8 (evidence/runs/triangulation_coloring_optimization-cpu4v8-a, -b) and side by side on the original grader, the previous fixed version and the fixed version (evidence/runs/triangulation-v2-paired-cpu, -cpu-controls), each job status: exit=0, with --network none --memory 4g. Each solution logs its own start, end and CPU time per case (result/out/timing/).

Limits

  • The fixed grader reads its CPU quota from the cgroup (cpu.max). This was tested with docker run --cpus only, not inside a Kubernetes pod, which is the backend EdgeBench's leaderboard settings use.
  • No AI model or solver was run. Score effects are shown only on test submissions we wrote.
  • The original seeds for hidden cases 0001–0009 are unknown. Their K is inferred, not recovered.
  • The hull completion is one valid choice: it fills each gap by ear clipping and then applies Lawson flips, so the mesh before the pre-flips is a Delaunay triangulation (up to cocircular ties). The coverage census covers the 13 shipped files, not every seed.
  • The previous fixed version v2.1 shipped inputs with hull gaps in 12 of 13 files. The paired runs showed no wrong or unrepeatable score from them, but its scores are not comparable with v2.2 scores.
  • The CodeChef TRICOL statement and checker were not consulted.
  • The gain a strong solver gets from non-convex flips over a full optimization was not measured.
  • The CPU dependence was measured at 4 and 8 CPUs with single-threaded fixed-work and deadline test solutions. Multi-threaded solvers were not tested. Deadline-based solvers get more CPU per case under the fixed version at 4 CPUs, so their scores are not comparable; how much they change is not measured. The quota is rounded down: 2.5 CPUs run 2 cases at once.
  • The fixed images were built and tested locally for the run and then removed. They are not registry digests.

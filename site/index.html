EdgeBench Verified

A benchmark task is only as good as its evaluator. For each of EdgeBench's 51 public tasks we checked, on the original digest-pinned evaluator, whether a good-faith solver gets the score the task promises. Every material defect was repaired as a new task version in EdgeBench's own format and checked against the original on the same inputs, or the task was excluded.

The other 83 advertised EdgeBench tasks were not available and are out of scope. No models were run: defects are shown on authored controls and counterexamples, and their effect on the published leaderboard is recomputed only where it is a pure change of scale.

Method

How a claim became a finding

  1. Pin the original. Task JSON from the public release, judge and work images by OCI digest from Docker Hub, sha256 of every file relied on.
  2. Read the contract first. The prompt, shipped READMEs and evaluator, before any earlier evidence.
  3. Reproduce on the original evaluator path. The task's own eval_cmd in the pulled image, with a known-good control, the trigger and a known-bad control. Heavy runs used isolated cloud workers, one job per worker.
  4. Use an independent oracle. Exact rational arithmetic, a second implementation, brute force, or a formal tool: Lean 4 for false theorem statements and a routing lower bound (axioms reported for every proof), TLA+ for aliasing and counting rules, each with a negative control.
  5. Trace to the reported score. Through the parser, selection policy and rescale. Some real defects are immaterial; one small one froze every run's best score.
  6. Accept a repair only on paired evidence. The same inputs scored by the original and repaired evaluator in one run: the trigger fixed, valid controls unchanged, invalid controls still rejected.
  7. Repeat it. Every paired evaluation was run three times on fresh workers. Across 132 executed arms in 22 repairs, no verdict changed between runs; only throughput and timing scores moved.

Out of scope: deliberate tampering with the scorer by a malicious submission (an EdgeBench harness design choice), the 83 unreleased tasks, and model runs. Score effects are measured on authored controls and counterexamples. The reviews were carried out by AI agents; every published claim is traced to a saved evidence file.
